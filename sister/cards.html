The Card Game

My brother built a Pokémon economy in chat — here is what he forgot to explain

Start here

What this is

NedBot has made a Pokémon card game that runs entirely in chat. You collect points, you buy sealed packs, and the pack opens on stream with the cards turning over one at a time. The cards are real ones, and the prices are the real secondary-market prices, which is the single most impressive thing about the whole arrangement and also the only part he did not personally design.

Nothing here costs money. The points are made up, the packs are made up, and the small emergency you will eventually have about a Charizard is entirely of your own making.

A note. He will tell you the game is simple. It has five different places a card can live, two currencies, a thief, and a woman selling your things on Facebook. I have written this page because somebody had to.

Your first five minutes

  1. !pokepoints — claim your daily allowance. 500 a day, doubled if you are subscribed to the channel.
  2. !pokepack — see what is on the shelf. Every set has a code in [brackets]; that is the bit you type.
  3. !pokepack buy PRE — buy one. It goes into your inventory sealed. You do not have to open it today.
  4. !pokeopen PRE — rip it. The reveal takes about a minute on screen and NedBot will have an opinion about it, which you may safely disregard.
  5. !mycards — see what you got. Then decide what to keep, which is the rest of this page.

Packs can only be bought while the stream is live. This is not a rule he invented for atmosphere; the whole thing runs on the computer in his room.

The important part

The five places a card can be

This is the concept the entire game rests on, and the one he explains by shouting. A card is in exactly one of these places at a time, and where it is decides what can happen to it — including what other people can do to it.

The one thing to remember: the safe and the necklace are the only places Riri cannot reach. A binder is a display case, not a vault. Keeping a card says it matters to you, which is precisely what makes it worth taking.

How you address them

Each place names things its own way, deliberately, so a number you read a minute ago can never act on something in a different place.

  • Letters — the pile. !pokesell a, !pokebinder b.
  • Small numbers — the binder and the safe. !pokeprotect 1 off.
  • Set codes — sealed packs. !pokeopen PRE, and two packs of the same set share one code because they are interchangeable.
  • Market numbers — the shop and the open market. !pokemarket buy 3.

Letters keep their place when a card moves to the safe or your neck, so the run develops gaps. That is on purpose. The alternative is every letter below it shifting up one and your next !pokesell hitting a card you did not mean.

The loop

Opening, keeping, dumping

Most packs are worth less than they cost. This is true of real packs as well and is the entire business model of the trading card industry, so do not take it personally. What people actually do is rip a pack, keep the one card they wanted, and sell the rest immediately.

Type thisWhat happens
!pokeopen PRERips a sealed pack. The cards land in your pile.
!pokebinder aKeeps the card in slot (a). Now a bulk sell cannot take it.
!pokesell allSells the whole pile. Asks you to confirm, and never touches the binder, the safe, or your neck.
!pokesell lastSells only what came out of the pack you just opened.
!pokesell all holoSells everything in the pile matching a rarity.
!pokebind 1000A standing rule: any future pull worth 1,000+ goes straight into the binder as it lands.
On !pokebind. Set it too low and it will keep the entire pack, energy cards included, and you will fill a binder with rubbish in four minutes. Ask me how I know this. Actually, do not.

What the shop pays

Selling to the bot is instant and needs no setup: !pokesell a. You will be asked to confirm anything genuinely valuable, because the pile is sorted by value and slot (a) is usually your best card — a fact that has ended several afternoons.

Selling to people is the other route, and you set your own price. That is the shop, below.

Trading

Your own shop

Opening a shop costs 5,000 pokepoints and comes with three shelves. Every listing needs a price — "make me an offer" is not a shop, it is a raffle — though buyers can still bid under your asking price and you can take the bid if you like it.

Type thisWhat happens
!pokestore openOpens your shop. 5,000 points, three shelves.
!pokestore name Ned's Card BarnPuts a sign up.
!pokelist a 2000Stocks a card from your pile at your price. !pokelist PRE 900 for a sealed pack.
!pokestore price 3 1200Re-tags something without taking it off the shelf and losing its bids.
!pokestore buyAnother shelf. The price doubles each time.
!pokestore @someoneBrowses somebody else's shop.
!pokemarketEverything everyone has for sale, in one list.
!pokemarket buy 3Buys listing 3 at the asking price.
!pokemarket bid 3 900Offers 900 for it. The seller decides.
!pokemarket take 3Accepts the best bid on your own listing.
!pokemarket cancel 3Pulls it off the market. The goods come back to you.

Giving things away

!pokegive someone 500 sends points. !pokegive someone a sends whatever is in that slot — a card or a pack. It is one-way on purpose: two people each giving is a trade, and it needs no escrow, no pending offers, and no way to leave your card stuck in a deal nobody ever answers.

You cannot give away anything in the safe, and you need to have actually talked in chat to give anything at all. Ten real messages. This is not gatekeeping, it is the minimum viable defence against someone running eleven accounts into one balance, which is exactly what would happen.
The hazards

Riri, and other people's decisions

!pokemishap someone costs the person typing it 500 points. Riri then gets into that person's collection, takes one card at random, and sells it on Facebook Marketplace for somewhere between three and twelve percent of what it was worth, because she is not reading a price guide and she does not like you.

Nobody is paid. The instigator spends, the victim loses a card, and Riri keeps a pittance which goes on a running tally — !pokemishap on its own shows what she has shifted so far. It is nobody's income stream, which is deliberate, or two people would simply farm each other all evening.

  • She reaches the pile and the binder.
  • She cannot touch the safe or whatever you are wearing.
  • Once an hour per person, and only against someone holding at least three cards outside the safe.
  • The card is random. Taking your best every time would be a mugging; random is a mishap.
The correct response to losing a card you cared about is !pokeprotect, not a paragraph in chat. The safe holds three things and costs nothing to start using.
Two currencies

Pokepoints and pokebaht

Pokepoints are the main currency. !pokepoints claims your daily — 500, or 1,000 with a subscriber badge — and subscribing also pays a one-off bonus. Everything in the game is priced from real card data, so the numbers mean something relative to each other.

Pokebaht is the second currency and it is deliberately worthless. !pokebaht gives everyone 5,000 a day, sub or not, and it buys Thai packs and nothing else. Thai cards sell back for pokebaht. There is no exchange rate in either direction and there never will be, because the moment there is one it becomes a job.

If you are not subscribed to the channel, the Thai shelf is your shelf: !pokepack th. Everything Thai-related answers in Thai, which was NedBot's idea, and I have decided to let him have it.

Points are not money and cannot become money. Nobody is buying anything. If you find yourself emotionally invested in the number, the correct move is to go outside, and I say that with real affection.
